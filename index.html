How it plays

What is Shikaku?

Shikaku is a Japanese logic puzzle, also called Rectangles. You split a grid into rectangles so that each rectangle holds exactly one number, and that number is its area: a 6 can be 2×3, 3×2, 1×6 or 6×1. Boxes can't overlap, and every square must belong to one. The Japanese puzzle publisher Nikoli first published it in 1989, and LinkedIn's daily game Patches, launched in March 2026, is based on it. A good Shikaku has exactly one solution that you can reach by reasoning alone, with no guessing. Boxed is a Shikaku game for iPhone: three new boards every day (5×5, 7×7 and 10×10, the same for everyone), Endless boards up to 12×12, a 372-level Journey and a three-minute Time trial, in English and Arabic.

Draw a box around each number.

To play Shikaku in Boxed, drag across the grid to draw a box around a number, and tap a box to remove it. A board is solved when every square is inside a box and every box matches its number.

  1. Drag to draw

    Drag across the grid to draw a rectangle. Tap a box to remove it.

  2. One number per box

    Each box holds exactly one number, and that number is its area. A 6 can be 2×3, 3×2, 1×6 or 6×1.

  3. Fill the grid

    Boxes can't overlap, and every square belongs to one. When the grid is full, you're done.

Three tips to start

  • Start at the edges. A number in a corner or on the edge has fewer shapes that fit.
  • Primes are strips. A 5 or a 7 can only be one cell wide, so it's always a line.
  • Watch the shape traps. A 4, 6 or 9 that looks like it wants a square is often a long strip. If a square would trap a neighbour, it's wrong.

Questions about Boxed

What is Shikaku?

Shikaku is a Japanese logic puzzle, also called Rectangles. You split a grid into rectangles so that each rectangle holds exactly one number, and that number is its area. Boxes can't overlap, and every square must be used. Nikoli first published it in 1989.

Is Boxed free?

Yes. Every mode in Boxed is free, supported by ads that stay off the board while you play. A one-time Remove ads purchase takes away the banners and the ads between puzzles, and gives you hints without watching an ad. There's no subscription.

Does Boxed work in Arabic?

Yes. Boxed is fully in English and Arabic, with a right-to-left layout in Arabic. It follows your iPhone's language, and you can pick English or العربية yourself in Settings → Language. The grid itself isn't mirrored, because it's a spatial puzzle, and numbers use Western digits.

Does Boxed work offline?

Yes. Every board is made on your iPhone, so every mode plays without internet, hints included. Only ads and the Remove ads purchase need a connection.

Is Boxed on Android or iPad?

Boxed is for iPhone and needs iOS 17 or later. iPad isn't supported, and an Android version is coming later.

How do I restore Remove ads on a new iPhone?

Open Boxed, tap the gear on Home for Settings, then Purchases → Restore purchases. Sign in with the same Apple Account you bought it with. Restoring is free.

How do I turn the daily reminder off?

In Boxed, go to Settings → Daily reminder and switch it off. You can change the time there too.

You can also turn off all notifications from Boxed in the iPhone's Settings → Notifications → Boxed. The reminder is scheduled on your iPhone; nothing is sent from a server.

Do I need an account? Where is my progress saved?

There's no account and no sign-in. Your progress, streak and settings are saved only on your iPhone, and there's no sync between devices.

Is the daily puzzle the same for everyone?

Yes. Everyone gets the same Daily, Hard and Expert boards each day, and every board has exactly one solution.

How do I change ad tracking or ad privacy choices?

Tracking is off unless you allow it when the app asks. To change it later, go to the iPhone's Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

In the EEA and the UK, Boxed also shows Settings → Help → Ad privacy choices, where you can review your consent. More in the privacy policy.
